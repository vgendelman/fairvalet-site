Why do valets cone off front spots?

Staging, curb control, and revenue incentives — and what fair practice looks like instead.

Valets often cone off front spots to stage cars, keep a clear curb for drop-off, or push guests toward paying for valet. The result for self-parkers is usually the same: the closest spaces are gone.

Common reasons

  • Staging. Staff want a buffer of empty spots for rapid handoff.
  • Curb control. Cones keep the driveway clear for continuous drop-off.
  • Revenue. Scarcer self-park options nudge more people into valet.
  • Optics. A neat “valet only” row can look premium from the street.

Some of those goals are operationally real. FairValet’s objection is when storage — not active drop-off — permanently claims the nearest spots that self-parkers would use.

A fairer default

Keep a short curb lane for drop-off and pickup. Store cars farther back. Leave ordinary nearby spaces open for people who park themselves. That’s the FairValet rule.
